## Cross-city factors to compare

A shared site-selection framework includes workforce, logistics, real estate, taxes and permits, utilities, and project timing. Which factors matter most depends on the facility type: logistics may be central for distribution, workforce access for manufacturing, and talent access for offices. See the [SelectUSA Investor Guide: Site Selection](https://www.trade.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/2025%20SelectUSA%20Investor%20Guide.pdf).

For a direct comparison, use common measures where available: sector employment and wages, business establishments, project type and location, housing and commercial property costs, utility capacity, and project delivery. The repository already covers some employment, wage, establishment, GDP, and announcement measures. It does not consistently measure skills supply, site readiness, utility capacity, housing affordability, incentive awards, or completed project outcomes; those remain research gaps.

## Notes

- “Atlanta” and the peer-city labels may refer to either city boundaries or metro areas; record the geographic scope for each source and measure.
- Company announcements are not the same as realized jobs, capital investment, or long-term retention.
- Regional economic-development organizations are useful sources for identifying priorities and reported assets. Pair their claims with public statistics and independent research when assessing outcomes.
