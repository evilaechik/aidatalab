# Economic and Business Dataset Context

Updated October 27, 2026.

These data support the goal of identifying where **Atlanta lags in business attraction, expansion and retention**, investigating the drivers of those gaps, and assessing possible incentive strategies. Atlanta and 49 comparison metros now have the same nine data files, covering employment, business activity, output, tax conditions and major corporate presence. All 50 entries in the supplied ranking list are covered. Collection does not by itself justify the peer group or establish incentive effects.

**450 data files are collected**, with nine files in each of 50 city folders. On October 27, [batches 1](collection_batch_01.md), [2](collection_batch_02.md) and [3](collection_batch_03.md) each added 90 files for ten missing metros; the explicitly authorized [final batch](collection_batch_04.md) added 99 files for the remaining eleven. Source programs, reference windows and per-dataset column order are preserved. All 39 metro folders that existed before the final batch remain unchanged. Dallas/Denver retain their October 15 verification dates, the seven October 21 additions retain theirs, and the 41 batch additions carry October 27 verification dates. **No entries in the [top-50 list](top_50_us_metro_areas_by_gdp.md) remain uncollected.** The final audit verified every ranking position exactly once, all 450 saved files against their manifests/dictionaries, and every available GDP sum. Source availability limits remain explicitly qualified below.

Regional measures describe metropolitan economies, rather than municipal boundaries. GDP retains individual counties and explicitly marked calculated metro totals. Tax observations are state-level. Raleigh excludes Durham; Miami includes Broward and Palm Beach. County GDP membership is documented in every city dictionary using the July 2023 OMB delineation; other programs retain their native geography and industry vintages.

Hartford's county equivalents are Capitol (09110) and Lower Connecticut River Valley (09130) planning regions; Bridgeport's are Greater Bridgeport (09120) and Western Connecticut (09190). **Their 2023 GDP is unavailable on those boundaries.** The [BEA February 5, 2026 release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gross-domestic-product-county-and-personal-income-county-2024) and CAGDP2 archive footnotes specify planning-region estimates from 2024 onward. All 2023 region-industry rows retain `(NA)` and `value_status=source_flag`. Only the complete 2024 metro sum is derived; no 2023 metro total is fabricated and historical-county GDP is not substituted for a different geography. The files retain both requested years, with unavailable 2023 observations explicitly marked.

The ranking list uses the older NY–NJ–PA and IL–IN–WI labels. July 2023 OMB membership is NY–NJ for New York and IL–IN for Chicago; the new folders follow that documented definition. Washington GDP retains BEA’s combined Virginia reporting areas: Fairfax/Fairfax city/Falls Church (51919), Prince William/Manassas/Manassas Park (51942), and Spotsylvania/Fredericksburg (51951). These combinations cover whole constituent counties/cities within the metro. The dictionary maps all 18 BEA reporting areas to all 23 OMB county equivalents; separate component GDP values are not fabricated.

The final Virginia metros follow the same combined-area rule. Virginia Beach retains James City/Williamsburg (51931) and York/Poquoson (51958), with 16 BEA reporting areas covering all 18 OMB equivalents. Richmond retains Dinwiddie/Colonial Heights/Petersburg (51918) and Prince George/Hopewell (51941), with 14 reporting areas covering all 17 OMB equivalents. All components lie wholly inside their target metros. Dictionaries map every source area to its components without double-counting.

Baltimore city and Baltimore County are separate county equivalents in the Baltimore GDP file. St. Louis city and St. Louis County are likewise separate in the St. Louis file; each is included once. Indianapolis uses the July 2023 title Indianapolis–Carmel–Greenwood and its 11 constituent counties, while other source programs retain their native titles and vintages.

**QCEW geography:** The [BLS area guide](https://www.bls.gov/cew/classifications/areas/area-guide.htm) identifies a definition change from February 2013 metro definitions through 2023 to July 2023 definitions from 2024 onward. Native geography remains intact; matching CBSA helpers do not establish constant boundaries. Cleveland's folder uses canonical July 2023 CBSA **17410**, with six counties including Ashtabula. Its QCEW 2023 rows retain **C1746**, former five-county CBSA 17460, while 2024–2025 retain **C1741**. This is a geography break, so the 2023–2024 change must not be interpreted as growth on unchanged boundaries. Its Census responses use 17410, and GDP and Fortune assignment use the documented July 2023 metro.

[BLS also documents Connecticut's 2023–2024 county-to-planning-region break](https://www.bls.gov/cew/classifications/areas/new-2024-connecticut-counties.htm). Hartford and Bridgeport retain native QCEW values on either side of that change; unchanged metro codes do not make their boundaries comparable. New Orleans GDP and Fortune assignment use the seven-parish July 2023 metro, excluding St. Tammany Parish, which belongs to Slidell–Mandeville–Covington (43640). Its older QCEW/FRED geography differs. No geographic backcast is applied and these changes must not be read as growth on fixed boundaries.

The annual windows intentionally match Dallas/Denver. The two Economic Census files retain the requested **2022 exception**; 2025 rankings and 2026 tax conditions are separately dated context. This is not a common multi-year panel across all programs or a claim to contain each publisher’s newest release.

**Ranking GDP crosscheck:** Nine batch 2 metros reproduce the selection list’s GDP at its displayed precision. Indianapolis’s collected 2024 county sum is **$209.8 billion**, while the list says $199.2 billion. The latter matches the discontinued [BEA/FRED series’ 2023 value](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NGMP26900) of $199,197.977 million, last updated December 4, 2024. The collected files preserve the February 5, 2026 CAGDP2 release for 2023–2024; Indianapolis’s revised 2023 sum is $197.6 billion. The supplied ranking file remains unchanged and controls collection order, rather than replacing official observations or their reference periods.

All ten batch 3 ranking amounts likewise match older 2023 BEA/FRED metro observations, last updated December 4, 2024, rather than the collected 2024 county sums. Each dictionary records its older series URL, value, reference year and update date alongside the collected 2024 total; the [batch 3 comparison table](collection_batch_03.md#ranking-gdp-reference-years) provides all ten. Cleveland also differs in geographic definition. The requested 2023–2024 source window is preserved, and these older observations are explanatory evidence rather than replacement data.

The final eleven ranking amounts also match the corresponding older 2023 BEA/FRED observations; the [final comparison table](collection_batch_04.md#ranking-gdp-reference-years) and dictionaries record them. Reference year, revisions and geography can all differ, especially for Connecticut and New Orleans. Ranking amounts are not replacements for the collected source values, and the supplied list remains unchanged.

The Economic Census files overlap, as do the two CBP files; do not add their values together or add industry/size totals to their components. GDP uses the county table **CAGDP2** because SAGDP2N is a state table. GDP, receipts and Census payroll are in **thousand USD**, while QCEW wages/pay are **USD**. Metro GDP sums are derived and must not be added to county rows. Preserve source flags and identifiers as text; a suppressed source zero is not an observed zero. No industry crosswalk was applied.

Charlotte’s tax file has **two rows**, North Carolina (2%) and South Carolina (5%); these are separate state regimes, not an averaged metro rate. Texas’s 0 encodes absence of corporate income tax and does not remove its franchise/margin tax. The other state rates follow the requested 2026 Tax Foundation table and do not measure effective tax burdens.

The 41 batch-added tax files also retain one row per July 2023 constituent state, including DC. For graduated schedules or qualifying surtaxes, the numeric field records the top published statutory rate, while `source_display` and notes preserve the schedule and conditions. Connecticut's 8.25% includes a qualifying 10% surtax on a 7.5% base; qualification depends on gross proceeds or combined-unitary-group status, not simply taxable income. Portland has separate Oregon and Washington rows; Oregon also imposes a gross receipts corporate activity tax. Washington state’s 0 explicitly means no corporate income tax and does not remove its gross receipts business and occupation tax. Ohio, Texas and Nevada are likewise encoded as 0 for corporate income tax, with their separate commercial activity, franchise/margin or commerce taxes explicitly noted. Separate state rates must not be averaged into a metro rate.

Fortune files use the **2025 edition**. The original nine cite public regional/Chamber rosters, primary reports and public indexed ranking entries. Batches 1–4 use the public CSV behind [Fortune’s 2025 headquarters map](https://interactives.fortune.com/f500-dataviz-2025/), assign its reported coordinates to Census 2023 CBSA polygons, and preserve reported municipalities. They retain a single publisher location per company rather than adding co-headquarters. Amazon therefore appears in Seattle rather than being added again for Arlington. Publisher-reported US locations need not be global principal executive offices, legal domicile or all co-headquarters. This is not an independent historical-address or current-HQ audit.

Sacramento and Salt Lake City have **zero matched 2025 publisher map locations**. Their Fortune files contain the same 17-column header and zero records, with manifest status `collected_no_publisher_locations`. The complete 500-company source and target polygons were checked; no placeholder company is fabricated. This result does not establish absence of offices, nearby-CSA headquarters or all corporate presence.

Blank `in_target_metro_as_verified` means **unknown**, not zero. Current-HQ verification remains incomplete across the 48 additions after Dallas/Denver; partial verified-current counts should not be compared. Seattle’s 11 publisher-mapped entries include a specifically flagged Lululemon location conflict: Fortune maps Sumner, while the [company’s 2025-filed Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1397187/000139718725000013/lulu-20250202.htm) places executive offices in Vancouver, Canada and identifies Sumner as a distribution center. Retain the source row for traceability but exclude it from independently established Seattle headquarters counts. Detroit’s nine publisher-mapped entries include a flagged Autoliv conflict: Fortune maps Auburn Hills, while its [2025-filed Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1034670/000095017025023852/alv-20241231.htm) identifies principal executive offices in Stockholm, Sweden. Exclude that disputed US location from independently established Detroit global-headquarters counts. Denver’s historical QVC row retains its documented Pennsylvania move. Atlanta’s fresh Chamber roster includes Newell Brands, yielding 15 metro entries; Aflac and Mohawk are excluded because they are outside the metro.
