# Step 3: Incentive Program Comparison Framework and Evidence Assessment

## Purpose and status

This document sets up a public-source comparison of Atlanta’s programs with those in Austin, Charlotte, Dallas, Denver, Houston, Nashville, Raleigh, and Miami. It is designed to answer: **Where a specific Atlanta business-attraction, expansion, or retention outcome is weak, what program approaches do relevant peer regions use, how are those programs structured, and what evidence shows they delivered results?**

The project’s weak industry/outcome segments should be established from Steps 1–2 before selecting peers or recommending a program. The public project files describe employment, establishment, wage, GDP, and selected project-announcement patterns, but the peer-metro baseline is not itself a program-effectiveness dataset. Therefore this framework does not claim that any named region performs well in a particular weak segment or that incentives caused regional performance.

This is a public-source research document. It does not use restricted/private-use folders. Program rules and appropriations change; verify effective dates and the governing agreement before coding a specific award.

## 1. Comparison framework

Use **one program version per row** and preserve the jurisdiction. A metro may contain separate city, county, state, utility, and workforce programs. Do not attribute a state program to a city unless the project’s location and funding record support that attribution.

| Dimension | What to record | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Program type | Tax credit/exemption, grant, loan, tax abatement/TIF/TAD, workforce training, site preparation, infrastructure, or housing-related support. | Statute, program rule, official guidelines, or signed project agreement. |
| Eligibility | Eligible applicant and project; new location vs existing employer; minimum jobs, wages, capital investment, lease/site, matching funds, and application timing. | Current official criteria and dated program version. |
| Industry and place targeting | Eligible or preferred industries, firm sizes, priority districts, distressed-area criteria, wage tiers, resident-hiring or equity goals. | Published scoring criteria and mapped eligible geographies. Record “not stated” where criteria are silent. |
| Award structure | Formula, per-job or percentage basis, cap, duration, tax offset/refundability, reimbursement, match, stacking, and payment timing. | Program rule plus actual award agreement; published maximum is not the same as awarded amount. |
| Jobs/investment requirements | Baseline definition, net-new job count, wage/benefit threshold, capex requirement, start date, maintenance period, and milestones. | Contractual benchmarks and baseline definition. Keep promised values separate from measured values. |
| Funding | Appropriation/source, authorized amount, available balance, award commitment, actual disbursement, and public cost. | Budget, audited statements, payment records, or program performance report. Do not substitute total project investment for public program spending. |
| Delivery | Application, approval, contract, construction/training, opening, annual verification, payment, amendment, cancellation, recapture. | Dated transaction records and project follow-up. |
| Accountability | Reporting frequency, independent verification, audits, clawbacks/repayment, public disclosure, sanctions, and sunset/review. | Agreement language, audit findings, monitoring reports, and clawback/payment history. |
| Outcomes | Actual net jobs and wages, resident hires, capex spent, space/capacity opened, business survival/retention, tax receipts, supplier effects, and distribution across neighborhoods. | Follow-up observations at consistent intervals (e.g., opening, 1, 2, 5 years) with source and verification date. |
| Additionality / causal evidence | Whether the project would have happened here without support; spillovers and net effects versus a credible comparison. | Evaluation design, runner-up/comparison projects, policy-change design, or transparent company/site-selection records. |

## 3. Evidence-quality assessment

Use this scale **for each claim**, not as an overall score for a region. A program can have high-quality written rules but weak outcome evidence.

| Grade | Evidence level | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Rules** | Current statute, ordinance, official program guide, application, or eligibility policy with effective date. | Program type, eligibility, targeting, structure, stated requirements and accountability language. | Whether the program was funded, used, paid, or effective. |
| **B — Award / commitment** | Board/council action, executed agreement, public award list, or official project record with amount and commitments. | A recipient was approved or contractually committed to deliver specified outcomes. | That the money was paid or the outcomes achieved. |
| **C — Delivery / payout** | Audited payment record, annual performance report, documented reimbursement, or verified milestone record. | Funds were actually disbursed and/or a milestone was checked. | Long-term business survival, net regional impact, or what would have happened without the incentive. |
| **D — Realized outcomes** | Follow-up, independently verified jobs/wages/capex/opening/retention, with baseline and follow-up dates. | The project delivered specified outcomes over a defined period. | Causal attribution to the incentive without a comparison design. |
| **E — Additionality / causal evaluation** | Credible comparison group, policy-change design, counterfactual, or well-documented runner-up analysis; methods and uncertainty disclosed. | Whether the program likely changed the decision or created net additional benefits, within design limits. | A universal effect for other firms/programs/regions. |

## 4. Analysis protocol and decision rules

1. **Name the weak segment from evidence first.** State the outcome, industry/firm type, geography, years, comparator group, and benchmark. “Atlanta is weak” must refer to a defined measure (for example, relative job growth, retention, wages, or realized announcements), not an impression.
2. **Select peers by same-segment performance.** A metro is a relevant program comparator only if it performs well on the same outcome and the comparison is reasonably aligned by industry and geography. Record why each peer was selected.
3. **Map program to barrier.** Match a program’s mechanism to a documented constraint: skills gap → training; site-readiness gap → infrastructure/site support; wage-quality gap → wage-conditioned credits; housing/commute barrier → housing/access policy. Do not assume a financial subsidy solves a non-financial bottleneck.
4. **Separate eligibility and commitment from receipt.** Keep `eligible`, `applied`, `offered`, `approved`, `contracted`, `paid`, `verified`, `recaptured` and `unknown/no record` as distinct statuses. A program ceiling is not a project award; an award is not a payment.
5. **Track outcomes as a timeline.** Preserve announcement commitments, baseline, actual opening, actual jobs/wages/capex, annual progress, and follow-up survival. Include failed, delayed, reduced, and canceled projects.
6. **Account for other location factors.** Add workforce, wage, commercial property, utilities, transport, housing/commute, permitting and market-access evidence. Company statements and alternative-site documentation are useful case evidence.
7. **Do not state causality without a credible counterfactual.** If only program descriptions, awards, and outcomes are available, report design and delivery descriptively. Say “associated with” or “the project received,” not “the incentive caused.”

| Record | Fields to collect |
|---|---|
| Program version | Program name/ID; administering jurisdiction; type; effective dates; eligibility; sectors/firm-size/geography targets; thresholds; formula; cap; term; local match; payment schedule; milestones; clawback; stacking; appropriation; official URL; retrieval date. |
| Project award | Project/company ID; industry; site address and city/metro; attraction/expansion/retention; prior local presence; alternative sites; program ID; application/offer/approval/contract dates; requested/approved/paid/recaptured amounts; decision record and URL. |
| Baseline/commitment | Pre-project jobs/payroll; committed net jobs, wage floor, investment, space, deadline, local/resident hiring or training targets; agreement URL. |
| Follow-up | Verified actual jobs, wages, resident hires, training, capex, site opening, tax receipts if available, payment, delay/reduction/cancellation, clawback, operating status at 1/2/5 years; source/date/verification method. |
| Comparison factors | Industry employment/wages; relevant worker supply and skills; site/real-estate readiness and costs; utility capacity/cost/timing; transport/access; housing/commute; permits; customer/supplier proximity; same definitions and time period across metros. |

## Limitations and assumptions

- This is a framework and first-pass public program scan, not a completed peer effectiveness ranking.
- The weak outcome/industry segments and peer set have not yet been validated in this document. Complete that step before making the program comparison substantive.
- The project’s existing public decision tracker contains an initial sample of announcements; it does not establish incentive receipt, full project delivery, or a representative city/metro success rate.
- State and local policy packages differ. Compare program function and actual project economics, not simply program names or nominal award caps.
- Public absence of an award record is not proof that no assistance was received. Code `not located` separately from `none`.
- Program descriptions and reports may be advocacy/administrative sources. Evaluate the records’ completeness and verification method, and use independent evaluations for causal questions.
